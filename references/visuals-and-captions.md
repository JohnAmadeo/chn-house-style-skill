## Footage is the source of truth

**Respect the recorded video's original composition. Do not apply fixed crops, zooms, or repositioning by default.**

- The composition chosen during filming takes precedence over framing descriptions in the blocking script. John may change his mind while shooting.
- A script label such as “medium close-up,” “full shot,” or “frame left” does not authorize reframing footage that was shot differently.
- Apply a punch-in only when that particular section explicitly calls for the effect, such as “crop in tighter” or a progressive punch-in build. Start from the recorded framing and return to it when the requested effect ends.
- Do not introduce a base crop under a permitted punch-in. An allowed punch-in on one line does not authorize cropping the rest of the clip or episode.
- Do not zoom or move the presenter to fit stickers, reduce a table/background, manufacture shot variety, or enforce a tidier composition. Adapt the graphics to the footage.
- Match the foreground cutout's timing and transform to the underlying footage. This registration must preserve the original framing too.
- Fitting footage to the delivery canvas is not an editorial punch-in. Preserve the complete recorded frame and its aspect ratio; do not silently use a cover/fill crop or stretch it.
- Example: if the script says “medium close-up” but the recorded take is a wider kitchen shot, retain the wider kitchen shot. If the next section explicitly says “crop in tighter” twice, apply only those two relative punch-ins there.
## Punch zooms

- **“Punch zoom” means zoom the complete visual composite together: recorded footage, aligned foreground matte, visible stickers/title cards, and captions.** Apply one synchronized zoom with a shared focal point; keep the layers registered. Return to the existing framing when the requested beat ends. Limit the effect to the requested beat and preserve surrounding scenes and user edits.
- **Add the CHN whoosh at the start of every requested punch zoom**, unless John explicitly asks for silence or a different sound. Use `audio-whoosh-chn.mp3` from the shared audio folder and reuse the approved punch-in gain (`0.55` unless that instance has separately approved tuning). Keep dialogue and music timing unchanged; use constant gain.
- Choose zoom amount and duration for the specific beat rather than treating an episode's values as universal presets. Inspect before, during, hold and reset frames; keep captions inside title-safe margins.

## Safe margins for all text

- **All text must stay inside the inner title-safe area**, including identifying title cards, ordinary captions, emphasis captions, scripture/source references and labels inside graphics. Use 10% clearance from every canvas edge (the inner 80%). On a 1080 × 1920 canvas the bounds are x108–972 and y192–1728; scale proportionally for other resolutions. Keep the complete title-card rectangle, including padding, inside these bounds, not only its text baseline. Artwork may extend beyond the safe area; text may not.
- **Verify actual rendered bounds after placement and animation.** Include inherited parent transforms, rotation, scaling, italic overhang and any outline. Checking an untransformed HTML box alone is insufficient. Inspect entrances, holds and the most extreme animation frame; reposition/reflow the text or its card rather than reframing John’s footage. Preserve the rule that identifying cards sit behind the presenter.
- For visual review, use `/Users/johnamadeo/Desktop/content/shared/safe-zone.png` as a temporary top overlay or use the editor’s title-safe guides. Match the canvas aspect ratio and use the inner title-safe boundary rather than the outer action-safe boundary. Coordinate checks against the same bounds are also acceptable. Remove any temporary overlay from the saved composition and exports after checking; never bake guides into the video. Do not export a video merely to check margins.

## People: title cards

- **Title cards always go behind John, just like stickers.** Composite real room footage → stickers and their identifying title cards → aligned foreground presenter matte → dialogue/emphasis captions. Put the whole title card, including its black box and white text, below the presenter in the actual stacking order; moving it away from his face is not a substitute. If his head, hair, hand or body overlaps a card, John must occlude it. Verify an overlap frame visually. Preserve the readable foreground treatment for dialogue/emphasis captions; those are not identifying title cards.
- **Give people mentioned in an episode a title card when introduced, unless they are figures from the Bible.** Biblical figures do not need these identifying cards. **Outside these criteria, do not add a title card unless John explicitly asks for one.** Do not independently add scripture-reference cards, artwork disclaimers such as “Later artistic illustration,” or other explanatory title cards.
- A title card is a compact overlay: a solid black rectangular box with plain white, centered sans-serif text, using the CHN/Metropolis family. Keep square corners, modest padding, a tight one- or two-line layout, and a concise identifying role/title. Include the correctly spelled name when needed, with the role below it, as in the current Andreas Köstenberger and Nero labels. Avoid decorative borders, outlined lettering, colored panels or a full-screen card.
- **Title-card text must match the font size of normal dialogue captions in the same episode** (currently 50 px at 1080 × 1920), rather than using smaller label text. Resize each card’s width and height to fit the actual text and line breaks with modest, balanced padding. Keep the complete text block centered and the full rectangle title-safe; reflow or reposition as needed for readability while retaining presenter layering. Do not shrink the font to fit an undersized box.
- **Text inside every title card must always be vertically centered**, including multiline names/roles and source-reference cards. Center the complete text block within the black rectangle, with equal top/bottom padding. Preserve horizontal centering too.
- **Implementation in HyperFrames/HTML:** give the card wrapper its intended width and height. Make the black card fill that wrapper with `width:100%;height:100%;box-sizing:border-box`, then use `display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:center;text-align:center` and symmetric padding. Wrap all text in one inner `<span>` so `<br>` line breaks remain part of a single centered block rather than separate flex items:

```html
<div class="title-card" style="width:100%;height:100%;box-sizing:border-box;display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:center;text-align:center;padding:10px 14px;">
  <span>Pliny’s Letter to<br>Emperor Trajan</span>
</div>
```

- Keep the existing black background, white text and CHN font styling on the card. Do not simulate vertical centering with blank lines, manual text translations, top padding alone, or a line-height equal to the card height; those fail when a card has multiple lines or is resized. Move the card and text together, preserving the current card placement and presenter layering where possible; resize the rectangle to fit the approved caption-sized text. Inspect the rendered card after fonts load and after resizing to confirm balanced space above and below the text.
- Position the label beside or directly beneath the associated portrait/sticker, so its subject is clear. Keep real footage visible, avoid covering John's face/body or dialogue captions, and maintain the established presenter/illustration layering. Time it with that person's image/introduction and reuse an available track.

## Emphasis captions: blocking script to edit

- **Omit sentence-ending full stops/periods from captions by default.** Do not carry them over merely because they appear in the blocking script. Preserve meaningful punctuation such as question marks, quotation boundaries, and periods within abbreviations.
- **Mark spoken quotation boundaries in captions.** Put the opening quotation mark on the first caption of a quotation and the closing quotation mark on the last, even when the quotation spans several caption clips. Do not repeat quotation marks on every intervening fragment or extend them into John’s own commentary. Match the actual spoken wording and preserve complete caption text. **Preserve ellipses exactly as shown in the active blocking script’s quotations.** They mark omissions from the quoted source, even when the spoken delivery is continuous; do not remove them as silence, replace them with commas/full stops, or infer their placement from transcription. Keep each ellipsis attached to the corresponding word or phrase when a quotation is split across caption clips, including any ellipsis immediately before a closing quotation mark. Audit quotation marks and ellipses together against the blocking script; for example, retain “a degenerate… cult” and “normal to… torture… slave-girls”.

- **Read the current Notion blocking script before styling emphasis captions.** Identify ALL CAPS words or phrases in the spoken script as intentional emphasis cues, and map them to the corresponding words and timing in the recorded delivery. Use the active blocking version and John's latest corrections; do not collect cues from abandoned script variants, examples, headings, production notes or ordinary acronyms such as ESV/NT.
- Translate those cues into large ALL CAPS emphasis captions in the established CHN6–8 style. Inspect [CHN 6](CHN6-contact-sheet.jpg), [CHN 7](CHN7-contact-sheet.jpg) and [CHN 8](CHN8-contact-sheet.jpg) for reference: e.g. ANCIENT/EPIC in CHN6, WEIRD/VICTORY/MANY in CHN7, and SPICY/RESURRECTION/RISE AGAIN in CHN8. Preserve spoken-word synchronization and the surrounding ordinary captions; do not capitalize an entire sentence merely because one word is emphasized.

Measure the rendered lettering, including italic overhang, rather than relying on the text-box width alone. At 1080 × 1920, keep the lettering horizontally between x108 and x972 and vertically between y192 and y1728. Choose line breaks deliberately and fit each phrase individually. Do not zoom/reposition the footage to accommodate the caption, and keep John's face readable.
