## Editing and sound

- For the **red pulse heartbeat effect**, use the [combined visual/audio recipe](red-pulse-heartbeat.md): recorded paired thumps, synchronized red vignette, constant gain and continuous phase across scene cuts.

For the recurring generated opening, follow [generated opening](generated-opening.md) first. Its original dialogue and continuous-shot timing take precedence over the general speed and boundary-trim defaults below.

- **Remove accidental dead air at every scene boundary before handoff.** Inspect the actual selected, retimed dialogue waveform at the start and end of every scene, including after noise isolation or footage replacements. Start on the first spoken sound, leaving only a small protective margin (typically 10–40 ms, or the nearest safe frame); remove unused take setup and trailing silence unless a pause is deliberately requested. Do not use ASR word timestamps or the caption start as the sole cut point: in CHN10, the first word was timestamped near zero while the actual speech began roughly 0.4–0.7 seconds later. Locate quiet consonants and breaths that belong to the word rather than cutting blindly at the largest peak or applying one fixed amplitude threshold. Verify the boundary against the recording so no syllable is clipped.
- **Final-scene ending: when John walks off screen, keep the footage through his complete exit and cut when he is fully out of frame, not immediately after the last spoken word.** Inspect the video to locate that endpoint; preserve the walk-off even though the dialogue has ended. This takes precedence over the general trailing-silence trim rule.
- **Ripple scene-boundary trims through every synchronized layer.** Advance source offsets for footage, foreground mattes and dialogue; shift captions, stickers, animation cues and SFX by the same amount, shorten the scene, and move following scenes and spanning music cues accordingly. Preserve current user edits and intentional pauses. A graphic whose entrance has already completed before the new in-point should begin settled, rather than replaying its entrance or introducing negative timeline offsets. Check the revised seam and verify speech starts promptly; do not rely on a static contact sheet or a passing layout check to catch silence. Audit every remaining scene boundary before calling the whole edit ready.
- **Export or render a video only when John explicitly requests an export.** Editing, fixes, footage replacements and graphics updates do not authorize a preview-video or final-video export. Keep changes in the editable project/browser editor and use still snapshots for review until he requests an export.
- **Store each episode’s original video footage in `/Users/johnamadeo/Desktop/content/chn<x>/footage/`**, for example `chn10/footage/`. Copy supplied Downloads/cloud footage into that folder, preserve original filenames and media, and point source manifests and preparation scripts there. Place reused footage in a clearly named subfolder, such as `footage/reused-chn9-intro/`. Derived cuts and foreground mattes may remain in the editing project’s asset folder.
- **Default recorded talking-head footage to 1.1× playback unless John states otherwise.** Preserve voice pitch, and retime the dialogue, captions, stickers, aligned presenter mattes, animations, music cues and sound effects together. Apply explicit speed exceptions as directed; CHN9’s AI opening hook, HOT TAKES insert and washing-feet beat retain their separately approved speeds.
- **When given multiple takes of the same scene or line, use the last valid on-camera take unless John explicitly specifies another take. Off-camera repetitions are not takes.** Exclude rehearsals, spoken reminders and repetitions after John has walked out of frame from talking-head take selection. Inspect the actual footage alongside the transcript to confirm he is on camera performing the line; a later timestamp alone does not make a repetition the preferred take. Use that take’s matching video and dialogue. Use off-camera speech only when John explicitly requests it as voiceover. Apply this selection rule before assembling or tightening the edit, and preserve complete words and thoughts when cutting.
- Use the familiar CHN introduction where requested. Reuse an earlier episode's introduction only when authorized or supplied for that purpose.
- The reflective punchline should generally remain one continuous take, with minimal graphics and no stickers. A requested scripture image is an exception.
- Established music direction: swing jazz for the hook/body, with deliberate silence or effects where appropriate; Agape for the reflective conclusion. Follow the current episode's track choices rather than replacing them arbitrarily.
- Sound effects support specified visual beats and must not obscure speech.
- **Reuse approved audio settings.** Once John has tuned a sound or music clip, reuse its approved source, source trim, constant gain, static effects and requested native fades for subsequent instances unless directed otherwise. If he asks for an exact match, duplicate those sound settings while preserving the new cue’s intended start time. Do not introduce hidden automation.
- **For CHN whooshes, use `/Users/johnamadeo/Desktop/content/shared/audio-whoosh-chn.mp3` unless John specifies another sound.** This is the identical short whoosh reused in CHN9 and CHN10 (0.338005 seconds; SHA-256 `91dc9d74d89fd5b2b85f13502df77b0694c20d2e7864863cb7c52643ee02cf2c`). Source provenance is recorded in `audio-whoosh-chn.source.json`; original library attribution remains unverified.
- **For CHN risers, always use `/Users/johnamadeo/Desktop/content/shared/audio-riser-powerful-world-view.mp3` unless John explicitly specifies another sound.** This is CapCut’s “A powerful world view _RISER” (effect ID `6817201709559842818`), reused from CHN9. Import the full source into the editing project and adjust its source in/out and timeline placement in the editor; do not pre-cut the asset. Align the payoff using the guidance below.
- **Fit risers to their intended visual payoff.** Locate the audible build, crest and tail in the source before choosing a trim or duration. Align the crest with the requested beat, scene end or reveal. Do not blindly use only the first few seconds of a longer riser or let an unrequested continuation spill into the next scene. Preserve a specifically requested full-length or manually positioned version; retime or trim only within the requested treatment.
- **Never use audio keyframes or time-varying audio automation.** Set loudness with each clip’s constant gain. Do not add GSAP volume tweens/sets, `data-automation` audio envelopes, or keyframes for volume, gain, effects or playback rate. For an explicitly requested fade-in or fade-out, use the editor’s native clip-edge fade handles (`data-fade-in` / `data-fade-out`) instead. Keep audio controls predictable: no hidden coded volume instructions that override the gain John adjusts. When reusing an audio clip, copy its approved constant gain and requested native fades without introducing automation.
- **Do not programmatically synthesize sound effects that can be downloaded from YouTube.** Search YouTube for `<thing> sound effect`; this is usually effective. Prefer an existing recorded/library effect, use John’s supplied URL when given, download the audio into `/Users/johnamadeo/Desktop/content/shared/` with a meaningful `audio-<description>` filename, and retain source URL/title/channel metadata. Copy the working asset into the episode project. Do not silently substitute generated noise or tones for a sourced effect.
- **Run dialogue through an ElevenLabs Voice Isolator noise-reduction/voice-isolation pass by default.** Use the successful CHN9 workflow: assemble the selected final dialogue first, submit only that short dialogue cut to ElevenLabs Voice Isolator (`POST /v1/audio-isolation`), then normalize the cleaned speech. Keep the original recording, raw isolation response and cleaned working audio; keep dialogue independent of added music/SFX. Verify output duration and speech alignment before replacing the active dialogue, and preserve the final edit’s approved speed and timing. This is speech isolation, not voice generation.
- Do not claim voice isolation succeeded when only normalization or ordinary denoising was performed. Report an unavailable cleanup step accurately.
- Preserve editable source timelines, media and separate audio layers. Verify decoded exports as well as native previews, including sticker occlusion, captions, cuts, duration and final encoded audio levels.
